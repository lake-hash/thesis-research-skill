# Review The Increment Before Shortening

Judge a dated statement against full earlier sources and its contemporaneous
reply/quote context, not against later posts or only generated summaries.

First record what is genuinely added to the thesis: a business fact that supplies
new evidence, a changed expectation, mechanism, risk, forecast, valuation basis
or operative/invalidation condition, or a correction of attribution or meaning. Preserve that
increment in the prose. A generic bullish summary can erase the very reason to
keep an update. Do not then classify the damaged summary as redundant.

An actual trade, planned hold, position size, realized result, price change or
victory recap is private trade/position history unless the same expression also
adds substantive evidence or reasoning about why the thesis holds, weakens or
changes. Do not turn a result into thesis validation merely because price moved in
the predicted direction.

Support, resistance, targets, stops, entry/exit levels, moving averages, chart
patterns, volume, momentum and positioning are private `technical_out_of_scope`
history in the current public product. A conditional sentence such as "below 188,
next support is 186" does not become a public update. Likewise,
reported revenue, guidance, backlog or price movement needs an explicit investment
implication or changed condition. Objective facts alone are not public updates.

Repeated conviction, old figures, marketing, jokes and ordinary price recaps may
be retained as sources without another visible update. A duplicate decision needs
specific earlier event IDs and inspectable supporting evidence. A new buy/sell
statement is not the same execution merely because it occurs on the same day.
Report gaps instead of hiding an event when earlier evidence cannot be recovered.

Do not let a legacy label such as `REAFFIRM` veto a reviewed increment. A purchase
timing clarification, holding period, corrected list omission or newly completed
milestone can be an update even if an older classifier called it reaffirmation.
Record the increment and review provenance separately from the frozen event type.
Pure repetition still needs a source-only decision; this is not blanket approval
of all reaffirmations.

Keep content judgment separate from export readiness. Missing IDs, tickers or
source dependencies are explicit blockers, not reasons to reject the statement.
Before handoff, reconcile approved/keep decisions against actual exported IDs,
including aliases and merged members. Test omissions as well as false inclusions.

Different pages of one document revision do not create sequential updates. Within
one author/company, combine them as one disclosure, retain distinct facts and all
page links, and preserve original events privately. Never merge companies merely
because they appear in the same letter. A substantive document revision is a
separate event.

Preserve the source's certainty: jokes do not establish rankings or trades;
retrospective purchase recollections are not purchases today; a request to revisit
a post is not an exit rule; an anecdote is not broad demand evidence. Missing
instrument identity or unresolved references belong in pending source review.

Resolve a terse reply through its actual parent and conversation root before
borrowing context from another post. "Yes and no" does not supply unstated pros,
cons or entry conditions. A numeric trade plan must not inherit a ticker merely
because its price resembles one-tenth of an index, or because another same-day
trade named it. Preserve the pending numeric plan until the instrument is proved.
Contemporary filings or publisher episode metadata may identify factual context;
keep that context separate from the author's estimates and do not claim to have
read inaccessible media. A financing-gap estimate is not a company disclosure.

Use explicit outcomes: revised update, already resolved, source-only attachment,
combined disclosure, hold, or retained pending review. Retain the prior wording
and the decision evidence. Source-only and held items must not be exported as
new_thesis/thesis_update product cards. Changing the main argument requires a
fresh primary-source check, not just a new opening sentence.

## Concrete Review Cases

- A Nokia expansion update should preserve a tenfold capacity increase and the
  expected delivery period, not collapse into "Nokia benefits from AI demand."
- An Ondas order update should retain both the $70 million amount and the four-week
  period when present in the source. Neither is interchangeable with "strong orders."
- A Shopify post about AI agents threatening its checkout button cannot acquire
  identity or payment arguments first introduced in a later post.
- When one author quotes another's general power concern and adds a concern about
  a specific data center, keep those two speakers' claims separate.
- "Bought another 50k" is not automatically 50,000 shares or $50,000. Resolve units
  from contemporary evidence or state that they were not specified; do not invent
  a transaction amount. Hold if the company or the action itself is unresolved.
